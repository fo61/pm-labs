# X Post Sample: Streamflow STREAM Burn

**Context.** On 23 September 2026, Streamflow Foundation burned 699,994,595 STREAM in a single on-chain transaction, reducing total supply from 1B to roughly 300M. The burn covered 100% of the Foundation allocation (locked and unlocked) plus a significant portion of founder and future-team allocation. Mint authority was revoked, so the burned supply cannot be reissued.

Sources: Streamflow Foundation announcement (23 Sep 2026); Solscan token record for STREAM. Note the Solscan link below is the token page; the single-transaction page should be substituted before posting if the bounty requires a transaction-level proof link.

## Thread version

1/ On Sept 23, @streamflow_fi burned 699,994,595 $STREAM in a single on-chain transaction. Total supply fell from 1B to ~300M. That is 70% gone, not locked, not vested, destroyed at the mint level via Solana's burn instruction. https://streamflow.foundation/

2/ What was burned? 100% of the Foundation's allocation, locked and unlocked, plus a significant portion of founder and future team allocation.

3/ Why burn instead of lock? A lock still depends on future good intentions. A burn does not. The transaction is public on Solscan, and mint authority is now null, so the burned supply cannot simply be reissued.

4/ What remains of the original 1B: 11.47% in vesting contracts, 11.08% freely circulating, 4.03% for current/future team, 3.42% in Active Staking Rewards. Existing user vesting schedules were not accelerated or cancelled.

5/ A burn does not create demand, and Streamflow itself says this is not a price promise. What it does remove is the largest forward supply variable. The remaining question is usage, revenue and staking. Verify everything here: https://streamflow.foundation/

- "70%" is measured against the original 1B total supply, not circulating supply
